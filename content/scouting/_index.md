## Scout-Led

Troop 242 is run by its Scouts. Youth leaders plan meetings, organize campouts, and teach skills to one another, while adult leaders provide guidance, mentorship, and support. Every Scout has the chance to take on real responsibility.

## Leadership Roles

Scouts serve in positions of responsibility within their patrol and the troop, including patrol leader, senior patrol leader, assistant senior patrol leader, troop guide, scribe, quartermaster, historian, librarian, chaplain aide, instructor, and outdoor ethics guide. These roles build confidence, teamwork, and accountability.

## Advancement

Scouts advance through a series of ranks, each building on the last:

1. **Scout** — learn the Scout Oath, Law, and the basics of troop life
2. **Tenderfoot** — first camping, cooking, and first aid skills
3. **Second Class** — navigation, outdoor skills, and service
4. **First Class** — mastery of core Scouting skills
5. **Star** — merit badges, service, and a leadership position
6. **Life** — continued leadership and service
7. **Eagle Scout** — the highest rank, capped by a Scout-led service project

## Merit Badges

With more than [130 merit badges](https://www.scouting.org/skills/merit-badges/all/) to choose from — from Archery and Astronomy to Chemistry and Citizenship — Scouts explore careers, hobbies, and life skills with the help of adult counselors. Certain badges are required for Eagle Scout.
